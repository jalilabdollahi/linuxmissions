- systemctl mock-boot prints what is started, in order; failures and ordering
  cycles show up there and in journalctl.
- systemctl cat app db-proxy, and systemctl list-dependencies app
- Wants=/Requires= pull another unit in; they do not order. Without After=
  (or Before=) systemd starts unordered units at the same time.
- Requires= also makes a stop (or failed start) of the dependency stop the unit
  that requires it. BindsTo= is the stricter form and PartOf= only
  propagates stops and restarts.
- A is After=B and B is After=A can never be satisfied: that is an ordering
  cycle, and systemd breaks it by dropping a job.
- man systemd.unit (Requires=, After=, Wants=); daemon-reload after editing.
