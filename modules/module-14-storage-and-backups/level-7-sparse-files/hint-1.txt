A file has two sizes: the length it claims (what a program reading it to the end would see) and the disk blocks that are actually allocated to it. Compare what ls -l reports with what du reports for both images.
For the copy: a sparse source copied byte by byte becomes a fully allocated file, unless the tool is told to leave holes. And the tool cannot create holes from "real" zero blocks unless it is told to look for them.
For the tar: a plain tar of a sparse file writes the holes out as zeros.
