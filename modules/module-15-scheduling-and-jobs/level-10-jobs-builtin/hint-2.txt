CMD &         start CMD as a background job: [1], [2], [3] ... and $! holds its PID
jobs -l       job numbers, PIDs and states
wait %2       wait for job 2 only; afterwards $? is job 2's exit status
wait PID      the same with a PID (save it right after the &: pid=$!)
wait          with no argument: waits for ALL jobs, but returns 0, not their statuses
Careful: as soon as bash has reported a job as "Done" or "Exit 3", its %n is gone
("no such job"), and shards can finish in any order. A PID saved from $! still works:
bash remembers the exit status of its recent background PIDs.
More: help wait, help jobs
