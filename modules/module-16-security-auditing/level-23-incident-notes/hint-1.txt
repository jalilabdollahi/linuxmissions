Start from the log: separate the noise (people who only failed, internal logins) from the one address
that got in, and see what it logged in with the second time. Then, for each place an attacker can plant something that
runs later or lets them back in (login keys, scheduled jobs, shell startup files, setuid programs), compare what is
there with what should be. Recently changed files are a good lead (`ls -l --time-style=full-iso`, `find -newer`).
