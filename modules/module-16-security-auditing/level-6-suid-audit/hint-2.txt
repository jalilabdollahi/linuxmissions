find can match "any of these permission bits": `-perm /6000` (setuid 4000 or setgid 2000).
Strip the leading `rootfs` so the paths look like the baseline, then keep only the lines
that are NOT in the baseline: `grep -vxFf FILE` reads patterns from FILE, treats them as
fixed strings (-F) and whole lines (-x), and inverts the match (-v).
  find rootfs -type f -perm /6000 | sed 's|^rootfs||' | sort > found.txt
Then `chmod u-s,g-s` removes exactly the two bits and nothing else.
More: man find, man grep, man chmod
