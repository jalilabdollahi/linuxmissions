- ps -eo ppid,pid,pcpu,etimes,args prints parent PID, CPU share and age of
  every process; man ps lists the output columns. pcpu is CPU time divided
  by lifetime, so a child that burns CPU all its short life shows a high value.
- Run it in a loop with a short sleep (sleep 0.1) 20-30 times and filter the
  lines with awk on the CPU column; the process name will not give the answer away.
- Count how often each parent PID appears among the heavy children:
  sort | uniq -c | sort -rn.
- top -b -n 1 -o %CPU is another view, but a single sample misses most children.
