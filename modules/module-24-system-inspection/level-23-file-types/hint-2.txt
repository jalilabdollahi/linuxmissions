`file -b FILE` describes the content without repeating the name;
`file -b --mime-type FILE` gives one short token such as `image/png`,
`application/gzip` or `text/x-shellscript`. `file -z` looks inside
compressed files (gzip, bzip2, xz) and describes what they contain.
Match on the mime type in a `case` statement: executables are
`application/x-pie-executable` (or x-executable), plain text is `text/plain`
or `text/csv`, and the other `text/...` types are the scripts. Remember to
quote "$f" and to print the name and class with a TAB (`printf '%s\t%s\n'`).
`xxd -l 8 FILE` shows the raw first bytes if you want to verify one.
