os-release is written in shell syntax, so a subshell can read it for you:
`( . fleet/HOST/etc/os-release; echo "$ID $VERSION_ID" )`. The shell follows
symlinks and removes quotes, so you don't have to. The subshell keeps the
variables from leaking into your session.
eol.csv knows Rocky 9.3 only as `rocky,9`: take the part of VERSION_ID before
the first dot (`${VERSION_ID%%.*}`), except for Ubuntu, whose "major" is the
YY.MM string. awk can look a key up in eol.csv, and ISO dates compare
correctly as plain strings.
