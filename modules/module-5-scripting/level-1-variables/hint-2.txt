- `$1` is the first argument. `bash greet.sh "Ada Lovelace"` passes ONE argument, because the quotes keep the two words together.
- `${1:-stranger}` gives the value of `$1`, or `stranger` if `$1` is empty or missing.
- Store it in a variable (`name=...`, no spaces around `=`) and print it inside double quotes: `echo "Hello, $name!"`.

Two ways to put the lines into greet.sh:
- `edit greet.sh` opens it in an editor; type the lines and save.
- or type a heredoc at the prompt. Everything up to a line that is exactly EOF goes into the file:
cat > greet.sh <<'EOF'
(the lines of the script)
EOF

Read more: `man bash`, section "Parameter Expansion".
