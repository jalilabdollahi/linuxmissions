`column -t` turns its input into a table: it pads every column to the width of its
longest value and separates columns with spaces.
By default it splits fields on whitespace; -s CHARS tells it which character(s)
separate the input fields instead.
Alternative: awk -F, with printf "%-10s ..." gives every column a fixed width
(%-10s = left-aligned, at least 10 characters wide).
Read more: man column, man awk (printf)
