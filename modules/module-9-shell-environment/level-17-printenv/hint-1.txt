`printenv` and `env` show the environment of YOUR shell. The worker is a
different process: it received its environment when it was started, and
nothing that happened to worker.env since then has reached it.

So: find the worker's process ID (careful, two processes have "worker" in
their command line), then look for the place where Linux exposes a process's
environment under /proc/<pid>/. What separates the entries in that file?
